
Perfume wholesale trading software brief
Instructions for staff and the software development team | Main selling market USA | 10 October 2026
Build a central trading platform that imports supplier Excel lists, matches perfume products by EAN or UPC, calculates deal profitability, tracks observed supply and prices, and matches stock to customer demand. Use Excel for importing and exporting; keep the authoritative records and history in a database.
1 Business questions the system must answer
Which supplier offers the lowest executable landed cost for an exact SKU and quantity delivered to our US destination?
Which customers want this SKU, at what price and quantity, and how strong is their demand?
How much can we sell profitably, how much cash is required, and how long until the cash returns?
Are prices rising or falling, is supply tightening, and are we holding stock that should be sold?
Which offers are fresh, verified, territory eligible and supported by genuine stock?
2 Core operating workflow
Upload list → map columns → validate barcodes and units → review exceptions → approve offers → compare landed costs → match customer demand → prepare purchase recommendation and customer quote → record orders, receipts, deliveries and payments → measure realised performance.
3 Essential rules
Keep original files, file versions, row references and upload timestamps. Reimporting the same file must not duplicate offers.
Preserve past prices. Each new supplier list is a dated snapshot; it must not overwrite history or be added to earlier stock snapshots.
Do not call the sum of supplier lists total market inventory. Display observed supplier availability, our own stock and estimated supply separately.
AI may extract notes and suggest matches. Staff must approve uncertain product matches, quantities, prices and buying decisions.
4 Deliverables required from the team
Provide a standard Excel template, data dictionary, database model, upload and review screens, trading dashboard, customer demand dashboard, calculation specification, working pilot, test results and staff operating guide. Agree measurable acceptance targets before full development.
Supplier Excel format and product matching
5 Standard supplier template
One row per supplier offer for a specific SKU, packaging condition, stock lot, location and quantity price tier. Use blank for unknown values, never zero. Keep the supplier’s original EAN and description.
Field group
Required columns
Source and dates
Supplier ID and name, offer ID, list date, valid until, last stock confirmation, source file and row
Product identity
Original EAN or UPC as text, internal SKU, brand, fragrance name, concentration, size in ml, gender or category, set contents
Packaging and condition
Retail pack or tester, with or without cap and box, sealed or unboxed, damage status, batch or lot reference, manufacture date if verified
Price and quantity
Currency, price, price basis per unit or case, units per case, available quantity, quantity unit, MOQ, order multiple, price tier minimum and maximum
Logistics and terms
Stock country and warehouse, country of origin, Incoterm and named place, ready date, lead time, payment terms, freight inclusions
Eligibility and evidence
Permitted destination and channels, US suitability status, authenticity documents, stock owner ID, unique lot reference, stock confidence
6 Matching logic
Store barcodes as text to retain leading zeros. Validate length and check digit. Create a canonical GTIN representation padded to 14 digits for valid GTINs, while preserving the source value.
Maintain an internal SKU and barcode alias table. Confirm unit versus case barcodes and packaging level; never equate a case barcode to a bottle without a verified units per case conversion.
Check brand, fragrance, concentration, size, set contents and condition after barcode matching. The same code on supplier lists does not prove the goods have identical packaging, condition or authenticity.
Route missing, conflicting or invalid barcodes to an exception queue. Description matching may suggest candidates but must not merge products automatically.
Separate retail packs, testers, gift sets, refills and damaged packaging in comparisons. Allow an approved comparison group for related products without merging their inventory.
Reference: GS1 GTIN guidance and Verified by GS1. Barcode identity checks support matching; they do not authenticate a physical stock lot.
Profitability and cash deployment
7 Compare executable offers in USD
Use a dated exchange rate expressed as USD per unit of source currency. Retain original currency, rate source, rate date and any booked rate. Convert case prices to bottle prices before comparing. Run quantity scenarios because MOQ, price tiers and freight change the result.
Metric
Calculation or instruction
Landed unit cost
Purchase price in USD + allocated inbound freight, insurance, duty, brokerage, handling, nonrecoverable taxes and inbound fees per sellable unit
Net unit revenue
Selling price less discounts, rebates, returns allowance and customer deductions
Gross profit and margin
Gross profit = net revenue − landed cost; gross margin % = gross profit ÷ net revenue
Deal contribution
Gross profit less outbound delivery, commission, transaction fees, storage, financing and expected credit loss
Contribution margin
Deal contribution ÷ net revenue; keep markup, calculated on cost, separate
Cash return
Cash received dates less actual cash paid dates. For partial payments, show outstanding balance and dated cash flows
Capital efficiency
Deal contribution ÷ defined deployed capital. For simple deals show annualised simple return = return × 365 ÷ cash cycle days; do not present it as guaranteed return
8 Calculation controls
Define each cost once. Respect Incoterm inclusions to prevent double counting. Allocate shipment costs by a documented driver such as chargeable weight, volume or value; allocated totals must reconcile to the shipment.
Recoverable taxes affect cash funding but should not be included as permanent cost. Track refundable deposits, supplier advances and customer deposits in cash flows.
Use broker-confirmed duty and import inputs for the specific origin, classification and date. Missing material costs must show incomplete profitability, not a false zero-cost result.
Store financing rate and the dates funded. For several payments or receipts, use daily funding balances; use XIRR only where dated cash flows support it.
Show conservative, base and upside cases for selling price, FX, freight, delay and sell-through. Include a price-drop and customer-default stress case.
9 Illustration only
Buy 1,000 bottles at $30. Inbound costs total $3,000: landed cost is $33 per bottle. Sell at $40: gross profit is $7,000 and gross margin 17.5%. Outbound and other deal costs total $2,000: contribution is $5,000 and contribution margin 12.5%. On $35,000 deployed capital, simple deal return is 14.29%. A 90-day cycle implies 57.94% simple annualised return, assuming repetition; it is not a forecast.
10 Buying recommendation output
Show supplier, exact SKU, executable quantity, customer matches, demand status, landed cost, suggested selling price, contribution, cash requirement, expected collection date and missing assumptions. Apply manager-set minimum margins, capital limits and approval rules.
Inventory visibility and market trends
11 Inventory by SKU and territory
Own stock: on hand, reserved, quarantined, available to sell and in transit, by warehouse and lot. Available to sell = usable on hand − reservations; do not include inbound units before receipt.
Supplier stock: latest valid confirmed quantity per distinct stock owner and lot. Show reported quantity, confirmation date, location, destination eligibility and confidence.
Avoid duplicate supply: several brokers can offer one owner’s lot. Link confirmed duplicates to one stock pool. Where ownership is unknown, flag possible overlap and show a range or unverified quantity separately.
Distinguish stock location from selling territory. Stock eligible for both USA and another market remains one physical pool and must not be summed twice in global totals.
Unknown quantity stays unknown. Keep firm stock, indicative availability, incoming shipments and quantity on request separate. Expired or stale offers leave active totals but remain in history.
Label the dashboard “Observed available supply” with source count, confirmation freshness and coverage. An estimate of total territory stock needs independent coverage evidence and a documented estimation method; do not extrapolate from supplier counts alone.
12 Trend dashboard per exact SKU
Plot 7, 30, 90 and 180-day supplier ask prices and comparable landed costs: minimum, median, range and number of independent stock owners. Keep actual purchase prices, actual wholesale sale prices, customer target prices and public retail prices as separate series.
Plot active confirmed stock, independent supplier count, stock-outs, inbound units, customer demand and our sell-through. Show date gaps rather than inventing observations.
Compare equivalent quantities, conditions, delivery terms and territories. Use a matched-supplier series where possible so changing supplier mix does not masquerade as a price trend.
If weighting prices by stock quantity, use only verified deduplicated quantities; show the unweighted median as well. Apply configurable freshness windows and outlier flags.
Build forecasts only after enough reliable history exists. Backtest against a simple baseline, show forecast ranges and account for seasonality, promotions and stock-outs.
13 Alerts and trading actions
Alert on new profitable offers matching demand, significant price changes, rising demand with falling supply, stale supplier confirmations, falling margins, aged inventory, overdue collections and expiring wishlists. Include the evidence and suggested action. Thresholds must be configurable by brand or SKU.
Separate confirmed unfilled orders, current wishlists and probability-weighted opportunities. Never add them blindly: the same customer requirement may appear in notes, a quote and an order.
Customer notes wishlists and sales workflow
14 Customer record and demand fields
Maintain one customer ID linked to company, contacts, US delivery destination, selling channels, account owner, agreed currency, payment terms, credit limit and purchasing history. Store each demand as a separate dated record.
Demand field
What staff must capture
Product and flexibility
Exact SKU or EAN, brand, fragrance, size, concentration, packaging requirement; approved substitutes if any
Commercial requirement
Quantity and unit, target price and currency, delivery basis, destination, needed by date, payment terms
Status and evidence
Wishlist, active request, quote, confirmed order, fulfilled, lost or expired; evidence link, meeting date, owner, last reconfirmation
Uncertainty
Explicit requirement or inferred interest, extraction confidence, match confidence, staff approval and expiry date
15 Plaud and reMarkable ingestion
Use a meeting naming convention: customer ID, meeting date and staff member. Confirm the customer identity before attaching a note. Preserve the original transcript or note and its source location.
Plaud: start with transcript and summary exports. Evaluate its documented Zapier, MCP or CLI options in a technical pilot against the actual company account; choose the supported route that meets access and reliability needs. Do not assume an unrestricted public API.
reMarkable: start with exported text or PDF. Its supported cloud integrations include Google Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive; exported cloud files may be PDF. Use handwriting conversion or OCR where necessary and validate SKU numbers carefully. Do not assume live notebook access through an API.
Extract product mentions, quantities, target prices, delivery deadlines, channel restrictions, objections, competitor quotes and follow-up tasks. Attach the exact source excerpt or page to each extracted claim.
Staff approve extracted demands before activation. If size, EAN, currency, units or price is unclear, keep it unresolved. Never invent a barcode, promise or quantity.
Merge recurring mentions into the same open requirement with version history. A note must not silently overwrite a confirmed order or create duplicate demand.
16 Turn notes into sales actions
When an approved supplier offer matches a customer requirement, calculate customer-specific delivered contribution and flag the opportunity. Prepare a quote with quantity, validity, delivery terms and payment terms for staff approval. Track follow-ups, acceptance and why a quote was lost.
Use notes to identify price sensitivity, preferred brands, unacceptable packaging and replenishment patterns. General brand interest may inform prospecting; it is not firm SKU demand. Customer quotes must never reveal supplier names or buying costs.
Build priorities controls and acceptance
17 Implementation sequence
Phase 1 — Data foundation: standard Excel template, product master, supplier mappings, validation queue, dated offers and upload audit log. Staff clean a representative sample before migration.
Phase 2 — Trading engine: landed costs, quantity tiers, profitability scenarios, own inventory, reservations, customer demand and approved quote exports.
Phase 3 — Intelligence: market price and supply charts, alerts, Plaud and reMarkable imports, reviewed note extraction and follow-up tasks.
Phase 4 — Advanced decisions: demand forecasts, purchasing optimisation and accounting or warehouse connections after foundational data is reliable.
18 Improve buying and selling decisions
Rank suppliers by executable landed cost, fill rate, on-time delivery, claims, authenticity evidence, payment terms and confirmation accuracy; cheapest quoted price alone is insufficient.
Rank opportunities by contribution, credible sell-through, cash cycle, customer credit and concentration risk. Cap speculative buying and exposure per SKU, brand, supplier and customer.
Calculate maximum buy price from conservative achievable selling price less all deal costs and required contribution. Calculate the customer-specific minimum sell price consistently.
Track inventory aging buckets of 0–30, 31–90, 91–180, 181–365 and over 365 days; show days of cover, stock turn, markdown exposure and profit by lot.
Track quote conversion, lost-sale reasons, realised versus expected margin, supplier fill rate, customer repeat purchases, overdue debt and cash deployment. Tie each KPI to a named owner.
19 Controls and ownership
Purchasing owns supplier confirmations; sales owns reviewed customer demands; accounts owns FX, cost inputs, collections and reconciliation; warehouse owns physical quantities and lots; a manager approves master data exceptions and buying limits. Give users role-based access, keep an audit log, back up data and test restoration. Restrict customer notes and commercial costs to authorised staff.
For each deal capture US destination and channel eligibility, lot traceability, packaging and label evidence, origin and import review status. Have the broker or appropriate adviser confirm current import requirements and perfume transport requirements before execution; this brief does not supply legal clearance.
20 Acceptance tests before launch
Valid UPC and EAN aliases match correctly; leading zeros survive Excel import; case barcodes, testers, gift sets and conflicting sizes do not merge incorrectly.
Duplicate file upload creates no extra offers; a new list preserves old prices; one stock pool offered by two brokers is counted once; unknown and stale stock are excluded from confirmed totals.
The $30 example reconciles; case conversion, MOQ, price tiers, FX, included freight, recoverable taxes, partial receipts and returns produce reviewed results.
A note with an unclear SKU goes to review. Repeated meeting notes do not duplicate demand; approved requirements trigger an eligible profitable match; expired requirements do not.
Stock reservations cannot oversell the same lot; actual purchase, sales and payment records reconcile with accounts; supplier costs do not appear on customer exports.

22 Pilot and handover
Use several real supplier formats, overlapping stock offers, different currencies, case prices, retail packs, testers and a sample of customer notes. Include completed deals so estimated calculations can be compared with actual outcomes. Agree the expected file sizes, user count, refresh frequency and import performance targets.
Before launch, demonstrate one complete workflow: import → validated SKU → confirmed stock → customer demand → landed margin → reviewed quote → order → receipt → sale → collection → realised margin. Deliver a documented exception process and a data export so we can move our records to another system if needed.
23 Definition of completion
The system is ready when staff can identify a profitable, executable buying opportunity for a specific customer without manually joining supplier lists, can trace every recommendation to its source and assumptions, and can reconcile inventory and realised profit to actual transactions.
Confidence: high in the proposed data and trading framework. Medium in the exact automation route for device integrations until it is tested on the company accounts. Market coverage and future prices remain dependent on the available evidence.
